# Content and claims rules

Website copy for food is regulated, and false trade claims damage a young business. In India, the relevant rules include FSSAI's Advertising and Claims Regulations, 2018. This file is a working guide, not legal advice. Have the client confirm regulated claims.

## Allowed without further proof

Statements from the client's profile about what the business does: bulk supply, private-label support, B2B sourcing, responsive support, long-term relationships.

## Needs evidence from the client

| Claim | Evidence required |
|---|---|
| FSSAI, APEDA, IEC, ISO, HACCP | Certificate copy, current date, correct legal entity |
| Organic | Valid organic certification |
| GI-tagged Mithila makhana | Proof that the supply qualifies |
| Gluten-free, vegan, no preservatives, no added sugar | Product specification or lab report, per product |
| Nutrition numbers | Lab report or supplier specification |
| Exporting to named countries | Real shipments |
| "Best", "No. 1", "highest quality" | Avoid entirely |

## Never write

- That any food treats, cures or prevents a disease
- Weight-loss, diabetes, heart-health or immunity promises
- Invented numbers such as "500+ clients" or "10 years of experience"
- Fake testimonials or client logos

## Careful wording

| Avoid | Prefer |
|---|---|
| "We manufacture" | "We source and supply" or "manufacturing coordination" |
| "Leading exporter" | "Supplying buyers in India and abroad" (only after Q14) |
| "Superfood that boosts immunity" | "A light, popped snack" |
| "All flavours available" | "Flavours customised to your brief", with confirmed examples |

"Saatvik" and "healthy" are brand language and tagline, so they may appear as such. Keep "healthy" away from specific product benefit claims.

## Voice

Clear, professional, specific and warm, with a focus on what the buyer gets. Replace adjectives with facts where facts exist. Use Indian English spelling to match the client (flavoured, customised). Use "makhana (fox nuts)" on first mention on a page.
