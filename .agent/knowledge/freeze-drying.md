# Freeze-drying explainer

The client asked for the process to be explained on the site. This is general industry knowledge. Have the client's supplier confirm it matches their process, and do not add temperatures or percentages without their data.

## The process

1. **Prepare:** ripe fruit is selected, washed, peeled if needed and cut.
2. **Freeze:** the fruit is frozen solid, locking water in place as ice.
3. **Primary drying:** under deep vacuum and gentle controlled heat, the ice turns straight into vapour without passing through a liquid stage. This is called sublimation.
4. **Secondary drying:** remaining bound moisture is removed.
5. **Pack:** the dry fruit is sealed quickly in moisture-barrier packaging, because it readily absorbs moisture from air.

## Why it matters to buyers

- The fruit keeps its shape, colour and much of its aroma better than with heat drying, because the water leaves without high heat.
- The texture is light and crisp.
- It is low-moisture and very light, which suits shelf-stable products and shipping.
- Sensible uses: snacking packs, cereals, baking, confectionery, beverage mixes and hotel or café use.

## Comparison for the page

| | Freeze-dried | Conventional dried |
|---|---|---|
| How water is removed | Sublimation under vacuum | Warm air |
| Texture | Light, crisp | Chewy, dense |
| Shape and colour | Largely preserved | Shrinks, often darkens |
| Typical cost | Higher | Lower |

Say "retains much of" rather than making absolute nutrition statements. Do not claim "no added sugar" or "100% fruit" unless the product specification confirms it.
