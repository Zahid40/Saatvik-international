# B2B and export knowledge

Research snapshot from 2026-11-06. Most sources are supplier blogs and marketplace listings, not government pages. Verify on dgft.gov.in, apeda.gov.in and fssai.gov.in, or with a customs broker, before relying on any of this.

## What serious buyers check

1. Is this a real business? (GST, address, domain email, licences)
2. Can it meet the specification? (grades, spec sheets, lab reports, samples)
3. Can it supply consistently? (MOQ, lead time, packaging, repeat supply)
4. Is it easy to deal with? (fast reply, clear process, WhatsApp, documents)

The site should answer these in that order. Offering samples, if the client agrees, is a strong call to action.

## Registrations commonly expected for food exporters

- GST: the client has this
- FSSAI licence: Central licence is commonly cited for exporters
- IEC from DGFT
- APEDA registration (RCMC)
- Phytosanitary certificate, depending on product and destination
- Shipment documents: certificate of origin, packing list, invoice, insurance
- Optional, depending on the buyer: organic, GI, HACCP, ISO 22000, Halal, Kosher

The client has confirmed only GST. Saatvik may rely on partners for some of these, so ask rather than assume (Q5).

## HS codes: unresolved

Sources conflict: 19041090, 08109090 and 20081990 all appear, a dedicated makhana code reportedly took effect in 2025, and one guide names 20081921 and 20081929. **Do not publish an HS code** until a customs broker or the current ITC-HS schedule confirms it.

## Competitor patterns

Competing suppliers commonly advertise GI-tagged Bihar makhana, 5/6/7 suta grades, FSSAI certification, lab testing, MOQs around 1 tonne, and packs of 5, 10 or 25 kg. Use this as a checklist of what buyers expect to see, not as Saatvik's data.
